## Round vs Rectangular Ducts

The shape of your ductwork matters more than most people think. Round and rectangular ducts handle airflow differently, cost different amounts, and fit into different spaces. Here is a complete comparison to help you choose the right shape for your project.

## Round Ducts: The Efficiency Champion

Round ducts are the most aerodynamically efficient shape for moving air. A circle provides the maximum cross sectional area for the least amount of material and surface contact.

**Advantages:**
- **Lower friction loss** due to smaller surface area relative to cross section
- **More uniform airflow** with less turbulence
- **Lower noise** at the same airflow rate
- **Stronger structurally** (resists collapse under negative pressure)
- **Easier to seal** with fewer joints
- **Less material per CFM** of capacity

**Disadvantages:**
- Takes more vertical and horizontal space (a 12 inch round duct needs 12 inches clearance in all directions)
- Harder to fit in tight spaces between joists and above ceilings
- Fewer size options for custom applications

## Rectangular Ducts: The Space Saver

Rectangular ducts can be made in virtually any width to height ratio, making them ideal for tight spaces where round ducts simply will not fit.

**Advantages:**
- **Fits in tight spaces** (can be made wide and flat for low ceiling cavities)
- **Custom sizing** for any application
- **Easier to fabricate** on site
- **Better for main trunk lines** in basements with limited overhead clearance

**Disadvantages:**
- **Higher friction loss** due to larger surface area for the same airflow capacity
- **More turbulence** especially at corners
- **Requires more material** per CFM of capacity
- **More joints to seal** increasing air leakage potential
- **Structural bracing** needed for large sizes to prevent bowing

## The Math: Equivalent Sizing

A round duct and a rectangular duct can carry the same airflow, but they are not the same dimensions. The ASHRAE equivalent diameter formula converts between them:

**De = 1.3 × (a × b)^0.625 / (a + b)^0.25**

Where a and b are the rectangular duct sides in inches.

For example:
- A **12 × 8** inch rectangular duct is equivalent to approximately a **10.4 inch** round duct
- A **20 × 10** inch rectangular duct is equivalent to approximately a **14.2 inch** round duct

## Friction Loss Comparison

The same airflow through round vs rectangular ducts produces different friction losses:

| Airflow | Round Size | Rect Equivalent | Friction Increase |
|---|---|---|---|
| 400 CFM | 10" round | 14" × 8" | ~15% higher |
| 800 CFM | 14" round | 20" × 10" | ~12% higher |
| 1200 CFM | 16" round | 24" × 10" | ~18% higher |

Rectangular ducts typically have 10% to 20% higher friction loss than their round equivalents due to the larger perimeter to area ratio.

## Noise Comparison

Round ducts produce less noise at the same velocity because:
1. Airflow is more uniform (no corners creating turbulence)
2. The smooth circular shape minimizes whistling
3. Structural vibration is lower

For noise sensitive applications like bedrooms and offices, round ducts are preferred whenever space allows.

## Cost Comparison

| Factor | Round | Rectangular |
|---|---|---|
| Material cost per CFM | Lower | Higher |
| Fabrication time | Faster (standard sizes) | Slower (custom fitting) |
| Installation labor | Moderate | Higher (more joints) |
| Insulation cost | Lower (less surface) | Higher (more surface) |
| Sealing effort | Less | More |

Overall, round ducts are typically **15% to 25% cheaper** for the same airflow capacity when space is not a constraint.

## When to Use Each Shape

### Use Round Ducts When:
- Space is not a limiting factor
- Noise control is important
- Branch runs to individual rooms
- Using flex duct connections
- Maximizing energy efficiency

### Use Rectangular Ducts When:
- Vertical clearance is limited (between floor joists, above drop ceilings)
- Main trunk lines in basements with low ceilings
- Large airflow volumes need to fit in constrained spaces
- Building architecture requires flat duct profiles

### Use Both (Most Common)
The best residential systems use rectangular ducts for the main trunk (where space is tight) and round ducts for branch runs (where efficiency matters most). This combines the space advantages of rectangular with the performance advantages of round.

## Why Proper Duct Sizing Matters

One of the most common mistakes in HVAC installation is improper duct sizing. Ducts that are too small restrict airflow, causing the system to work harder and increasing energy costs. Ducts that are too large waste materials and can lead to low air velocity, which may cause temperature stratification.

## The Basics of Duct Sizing

When sizing ducts, three primary factors come into play:

1. **Airflow (CFM)** — The volume of air that needs to be delivered to each room
2. **Friction Rate** — The pressure drop per 100 feet of duct, measured in inches of water gauge (in/wg)
3. **Velocity** — The speed of air moving through the duct, measured in feet per minute (FPM)

## Residential Friction Rates

For most residential applications, the recommended friction rate is **0.08 in/wg per 100 feet** for supply ducts. Return ducts typically use a slightly lower rate of **0.06 in/wg per 100 feet**.

| Application | Friction Rate |
|---|---|
| Residential Supply | 0.08 in/wg |
| Residential Return | 0.06 in/wg |
| Commercial | 0.08 – 0.20 in/wg |

To determine the required duct diameter for a given CFM and friction rate, use the ASHRAE equal friction method. Our HVAC Duct Calculator automates this process — simply enter your parameters and get instant results.

## Tips for Residential Duct Design

- Always account for fittings (bends, tees, transitions) by adding equivalent lengths
- Keep duct runs as short and straight as possible
- Use smooth-wall metal ducts for main trunk lines
- Insulate ducts in unconditioned spaces
- Seal all joints to prevent air leakage
